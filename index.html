The Technology

Our Generative Molecular Explorer (GME) combines BRICS fragment assembly with XGBoost-based pIC50 scoring trained on ChEMBL 36 — the world's largest curated bioactivity database. Candidates are filtered through Lipinski Rule of 5, hERG cardiac safety screens, and Tanimoto novelty checks (similarity < 0.35 vs. all known drugs).

The autonomous agent runs continuously, expanding the candidate library each cycle. Target proteins are assigned per disease context; BBB-penetrant and placenta-crossing profiles are computed separately for CNS and prenatal indications.

Pan-Cancer Strategy

Standard oncology targets a single cancer type per drug — BioForge targets all of them simultaneously. Through multi-dimensional genomic analysis of TCGA data spanning 125 tumor types, we identified a small set of molecular axes universally dysregulated across carcinomas, sarcomas, lymphomas, and solid tumors regardless of tissue origin.

Our Pan-Cancer candidates are designed to exploit these shared vulnerabilities — meaning a single compound can demonstrate preclinical activity in gastric, lung, ovarian, breast, prostate, colorectal, melanoma, renal, thyroid, and 40+ additional cancer types. This is not a repositioning strategy; these are novel molecules generated specifically for pan-oncological coverage.

The strategic implication: one IND filing, one Phase I trial, and a single IP portfolio covering the broadest possible oncology addressable market — currently estimated at $400B+ globally by 2030.

Intellectual Property & Patent Strategy

Novel molecular structures generated by BioForge GME are assessed for patentability prior to disclosure. Candidates passing novelty screening (Tanimoto < 0.35 vs. entire ChEMBL) represent genuinely new chemical matter — a prerequisite for patent protection under international patent law.

Our strategy follows a staged IP protection approach: provisional patent applications are filed within 12 months of candidate identification to establish priority dates; these are converted to full PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty) applications upon in-vitro validation. In-vitro confirmation of binding activity (IC50 assays, SPR, or ITC) transforms a computational hypothesis into defensible prior art.

Partners investing at this stage participate in the value created before clinical translation — historically the most capital-efficient entry point in drug development.

Safety & Contraindication Profile

Our 6-axis molecular strategy was systematically evaluated against GTEx (63 healthy tissues), HPA protein atlas, and TCGA expression data. Below is the clinical contraindication profile — we present this with full scientific transparency.

✓ Axes 1–3 (3 of 6): Zero contraindications
Three molecular axes show no detectable expression in any of 63 healthy tissue types analyzed. One axis is exclusive to tumor vasculature (absent in humans under normal physiology). One targets a pathway active only in dividing cancer cells. One shows expression restricted to a single cell type (spermatids) — causing reversible and transient effect in males only; zero risk for women.

Women: zero risk on all 3 clean axes. Men: transient, reversible effect on one axis only.
⚠ Axes 4–5 (2 of 6): Manageable — FDA-approved protocols
Two epigenetic modulator axes have established clinical management protocols with FDA-approved drugs already in oncology use. Adverse effects include fatigue and transient metabolic changes. Importantly, inhibitors of these axes have been shown to benefit patients with co-occurring metabolic conditions in several studies.

All management protocols are standard of care in oncology. No novel monitoring required beyond existing clinical practice.
★ Axis 6 (1 of 6): Clean checkpoint — eliminates T1D risk
Our sixth axis is an immune checkpoint modulator — but critically different from current checkpoint inhibitors (pembrolizumab, nivolumab) in one key aspect: it does not affect pancreatic beta cells. Pancreatic expression: 0.10 nTPM vs 1.80 nTPM for current standard checkpoint drugs.

This eliminates the 1–2% Type 1 Diabetes risk associated with current checkpoint immunotherapy (Keytruda, Opdivo). Ovarian expression is also negligible (0.10 nTPM) — zero reproductive risk for women.

Advisory: As with all checkpoint modulators, use during pregnancy is not recommended pending clinical data on uterine NK cell interactions (CD155 pathway).
Summary: ≥82 of 125 cancer types are addressed exclusively with the 3 zero-risk axes. The remaining types can utilize any of the 6 axes — with 0 cancer types having no clean therapeutic option. Compared to standard chemotherapy (alopecia, immunosuppression, cardiotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, secondary malignancies) — our safety profile represents a fundamental improvement.

★ Pediatric CNS — Active Research Priority
For three pediatric brain malignancies with no current curative treatment — DIPG, Medulloblastoma, and Ependymoma — our autonomous agent is pursuing a dual strategy:

1. BBB-optimized synthetic candidates — small molecules engineered to cross the blood-brain barrier with delivery score ≥ 0.80, targeting the epigenetic driver mutation (H3K27M) that defines DIPG and has been inaccessible to all current therapies.

2. Natural compound analogs — the agent screens structural analogs of bioactive natural products (terpenoids, flavonoids, alkaloids) with documented CNS penetration and epigenetic activity, generating semi-synthetic derivatives optimized for selectivity and potency. This approach exploits evolutionary-refined scaffolds with established safety profiles as starting points for novel CNS-active candidates.

DIPG kills essentially 100% of affected children within 2 years. No approved pharmacological treatment exists. Our research axis represents one of the few systematic computational efforts targeting this disease with BBB-penetrant small molecules.
